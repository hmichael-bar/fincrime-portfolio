## Key judgments

- **Cyprus is a deliberate meeting point for rival intelligence services.** It sits roughly 200 km from Lebanon, Syria and Israel. It hosts British sovereign base areas, and it has a large Israeli, Russian and Middle Eastern business community. That makes it both an operating base and a target. Since 2021 at least three alleged Iranian plots against Israelis on the island have been publicly disrupted, the latest in December 2023 in a joint operation with Mossad [1][2].
- **Commercial surveillance links the island's intelligence story to its compliance risk.** The 2019 "spy van" case in Larnaca involved a company tied to Tal Dilian. In March 2024 OFAC designated Dilian and his Intellexa Consortium over the Predator spyware [3][4]. The same corporate-services infrastructure that enables offshore finance also hosted surveillance vendors.
- **The financial-services sector remains Cyprus's main AML exposure.** Two leaks set out the pattern. *Cyprus Confidential* (ICIJ, November 2023) showed Cypriot providers restructuring assets for Russian clients as sanctions approached [5][6]. The 2020 *Cyprus Papers* exposed high-risk buyers of "golden passports" [7].
- **The occupied north is a parallel, less-regulated jurisdiction.** It has an unregulated casino sector and an offshore banking sector, and no direct FATF or EU AML coverage. US and Cypriot sources describe it as a channel for drug proceeds and contraband [8][9].
- **Drug flows are rising, and the networks behind them overlap with fraud and smuggling.** Cocaine seizures in the south more than doubled in the first half of 2026, and Captagon appeared for the first time in volume [10]. A November 2025 organised-crime index flagged "state-embedded" actors and foreign criminal groups [11].

*Confidence:* moderate. The core events are well documented by government statements, OFAC actions and multi-outlet investigations. Claims about the north rely partly on advocacy-leaning sources and are flagged as such.

## 1. Why Cyprus attracts intelligence services

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Geography | The closest EU state to the Levant, about 30 minutes' flight from Beirut and Tel Aviv. It is a staging point for Lebanon and Syria evacuations and a listening post. |
| UK Sovereign Base Areas | RAF Akrotiri and Dhekelia are UK territory with long-standing signals-intelligence facilities, which makes the island strategically valuable to Western services. |
| Diaspora and business communities | Large Russian, Israeli, Lebanese and Iranian business presences give cover for collection and recruitment, and create targets. |
| Corporate services | Fast company formation, nominee directors and an English-common-law system make Cyprus attractive for front companies, including surveillance vendors. |
| Division of the island | The Turkish-controlled north sits outside EU law enforcement reach and is used as a gap in the net. |

## 2. Documented intelligence activity

### Iran and its proxies against Israeli targets
- **December 2023:** Cypriot authorities arrested two Iranian nationals described as having IRGC ties. They allegedly planned to target an Israeli businessman living on the island. The Cypriot government and Mossad announced the arrests jointly, and Iran denied involvement. It was reported as the third such plot in just over a year [1][2].
- **Earlier pattern:** Hezbollah operatives were convicted in Cyprus in 2013 for surveilling Israeli tourist targets, and in 2015 for storing ammonium nitrate in Larnaca. In June 2024 Hezbollah's leadership publicly threatened Cyprus over any use of its territory by Israel.

**Analytic note:** Cyprus combines a large, visible Israeli presence with easy entry and asylum routes. This makes it a low-cost venue for Iranian targeting operations, often carried out by hired criminals rather than trained officers. That criminal outsourcing is the point where intelligence activity and organised crime overlap.

### Commercial spyware: the Larnaca "spy van" and Predator
- **2019:** Cypriot police seized a surveillance van operated by WiSpear after its capabilities were showcased to the press. The company was linked to former Israeli intelligence officer Tal Dilian [4].
- **March 2024:** OFAC designated Dilian, a corporate off-shoring specialist and several entities in the Intellexa Consortium. They included Greece-based Intellexa S.A. and North Macedonia-based Cytrox, the developer of Predator. Further designations followed in September 2024 [3].
- **Greece link:** Predator is at the centre of Greece's 2022 wiretapping scandal, which involved journalists and politicians and drew in Greek companies and senior government figures [12].

**Compliance relevance:** The Intellexa structure spread across Cyprus, Greece, Ireland, Hungary and North Macedonia. It is a textbook multi-jurisdiction corporate web. Banks and corporate service providers with exposure to surveillance or "lawful intercept" vendors should screen beneficial owners against the 2024 designations and apply the OFAC 50 Percent Rule to affiliates.

## 3. Fraud and money laundering

### The golden passport programme (2007–2020)
Al Jazeera's *Cyprus Papers* leak named about 2,500 people who bought citizenship, including criminals and politically exposed persons. A follow-up undercover film in October 2020 showed the House Speaker and an MP apparently willing to help a fake applicant with a criminal record. The government abolished the programme from November 2020 and has since revoked more than 200 passports [7].

### Cyprus Confidential (2023)
More than 3.6 million documents leaked from Cypriot service providers showed firms, including PwC Cyprus, restructuring Russian clients' holdings as sanctions approached. Reporting counts dozens of clients later sanctioned by the EU, UK, US or Ukraine [5][6]. The Cypriot government said it had cooperated with sanctions enforcement.

### Typologies observed
| Typology | How it works in Cyprus |
|---|---|
| Shell and layered holding companies | Cypriot holding companies sit between assets and beneficial owners, often with nominee directors and trusts. |
| Pre-sanction restructuring | Assets are moved to relatives or new trusts just before designation, for example the reported transfer of a $1.4bn stake to a partner [5]. |
| Real estate and investment migration | Property purchases were used to qualify for residency or citizenship, and are linked to inflows of funds from unclear sources. |
| Casino-based laundering (north) | Cash-intensive casinos that can pay fixed licence fees create opacity, and illicit proceeds from Istanbul are reported [8][9]. |
| Professional enablers | Lawyers, accountants and corporate service providers act as gatekeepers, a vulnerability flagged repeatedly in leak investigations. |

## 4. Drugs and contraband crossover

- **Rising seizures:** Police reported 71.5 kg of cocaine seized in the first half of 2026, against 30.2 kg in the same period of 2025. They also seized 1,181 Captagon tablets, against 32 the year before, plus high-purity cocaine and concern about possible conversion labs [10].
- **Port routes:** Past cases include controlled deliveries of cocaine hidden in fruit containers arriving at Limassol port, run jointly with Greek and UK base-area police.
- **The north as a hub:** A November 2025 organised-crime assessment described the north as a hub for arms trafficking, excise and fuel smuggling, drug trafficking and laundering through casinos [8][11]. Halil Falyalı was a casino owner in the north who was convicted in absentia in the US on drug and laundering charges. He was assassinated in February 2022, which showed how deeply organised crime is embedded in the casino sector [9].
- **Captagon nexus:** Captagon is closely tied to the Assad-era Syrian state and Hezbollah-linked networks. Its arrival in Cyprus suggests the island is being tested as a transit point toward the EU and the Gulf. This is an *assessment* and should be monitored.

**Analytic note:** The same infrastructure serves drugs, contraband and sanctions evasion: ports, corporate shells, cash-intensive businesses and a porous Green Line. A network moving cigarettes or fuel north to south can move cocaine or cash using the same people.

## 5. Red flags for KYC and EDD teams

- Cypriot holding companies whose directors are professional nominees, with ultimate beneficial owners in Russia, Iran, Lebanon or Turkey.
- Ownership transfers to family members or new trusts between late 2021 and 2022, or shortly before any new sanctions package.
- Payments involving casinos, gaming or "hospitality" companies in the north, or banks there.
- Customers who acquired Cypriot citizenship through the 2007–2020 investment programme, particularly where the passport was later revoked.
- Exposure to surveillance, "cyber intelligence" or lawful-intercept vendors with ties to Intellexa-linked jurisdictions.
- Import/export companies trading fruit, food or construction goods through Limassol with Latin American or Levantine counterparties and thin trade histories.

## 6. Intelligence gaps

- How much drug-trade money is laundered through the north's casinos versus the south's real-estate market.
- Whether Captagon seizures reflect transit to the EU and Gulf or a domestic market.
- The outcome of the 2026 "mafia state" investigation into alleged state-embedded criminal actors [11].
