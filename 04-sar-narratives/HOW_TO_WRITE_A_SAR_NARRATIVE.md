# How to Write a SAR Narrative: One-Page Guide

> Study guide based on FinCEN's *Guidance on Preparing a Complete & Sufficient SAR Narrative* and the SAR form instructions. All examples in this folder are fictional.

**Why it matters:** the narrative (Part V) is the only free-text part of a SAR. Law enforcement often reads nothing else. If a detective cannot understand the case from the narrative alone, the SAR has failed.

## Structure: three parts
1. **Introduction** (1 paragraph). Why you are filing; the subject; the type of suspicious activity; the total amount and date range; how it was detected; whether it is an initial or continuing report (and the prior SAR's BSA ID if continuing).
2. **Body** (chronological). Facts in date order: who did what, when, where, how much, and through which accounts and counterparties. Group repetitive transactions ("five deposits totalling $41,670 on 04/24 to 04/26") and give the exact figures.
3. **Conclusion** (1 paragraph). Why the activity is suspicious, stated factually against the customer profile; actions the bank took (enhanced monitoring, 314(b), account closure); that documents are retained and available; a contact point.

## The 5 Ws + How checklist
| | Include |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Subject(s), occupation or business, relationship to the bank, other parties (counterparties, beneficiaries, depositors) |
| **What** | Instruments and products (cash, wire, ACH, P2P, crypto), amounts, accounts |
| **When** | Exact dates and the overall date range |
| **Where** | Branches (city, state), counterparty banks and countries |
| **Why** | What makes it suspicious, compared with the customer's expected activity |
| **How** | The method (structuring across branches, pass-through, third-party payments) |

## Style rules
- Write in plain English, past tense, third person. Spell out acronyms the first time you use them.
- State facts, not opinions or guesses: "deposited $9,920 at the Manhattan branch", not "laundered drug money".
- Use exact amounts and dates. Make sure totals add up to the amounts in the form fields.
- Include advisory key terms when they apply (for example `EFE FIN-2022-A002` for elder financial exploitation, `FIN-2022-RUSSIABIS` for Russia-related export-control evasion).
- Keep it self-contained. Attachments are not read; documents are kept by the bank, so say they are available.

## Common mistakes
1. **"See attached."** Spreadsheets are not part of the filing. Summarise the transactions in the text.
2. **Jargon and internal codes** ("R01 hit, TM L2 escalated per SOP 4.2"). Investigators outside the bank do not know these.
3. **Opinions and legal conclusions** ("the subject is a drug dealer"). Describe what happened and why it is unusual.
4. **Missing the "why".** A list of transactions without an explanation of what makes them suspicious.
5. **No customer context.** Without occupation and expected activity, a reader cannot see the mismatch.
6. **Wrong numbers.** Narrative totals that do not match the form or the tables.
7. **Not in date order.** Jumping between dates makes the pattern hard to follow.
8. **Too vague** ("multiple large cash deposits over several months"). Give the count, amounts and dates.
9. **Leaving out actions taken.** Say what the bank did (monitoring, closure, 314(b)) and where documents are held.
10. **Tipping off.** Nothing about the SAR may be shared with the subject; never write that the customer was told.

## Before / after
- ❌ *"Customer did a lot of structuring and moved money out. Suspicious. See attached spreadsheet."*
- ✅ *"Between 02/03/2026 and 06/14/2026 the subject made 14 cash deposits totalling $126,340.00, each between $8,170.00 and $9,920.00, at eight branches in NJ and NY. No deposit exceeded $10,000. Within three days of each group of deposits the subject sent the funds by ACH to her account at another bank."*
