**Rule of thumb:** no single flag proves evasion. Escalate when **two or more** flags line up, or when one flag touches a **listed party**. Always ask: *does the price, product, route, payer and owner make sense together?*

| | **1 · Shadow fleet oil** | **2 · TBML via Gulf free zones** | **3 · Crypto off-ramps** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Owner/manager formed in the last 12–24 months; single-ship companies; buyer of a 15+-year-old tanker paying cash | "General trading" LLC/FZE formed after Feb 2022; flexi-desk or mass address; nominee directors | OTC desk, P2P trader or VASP in a high-risk jurisdiction; nested exchange; individuals acting as cash-out mules |
| **What** | Crude or products of Russian, Iranian or Venezuelan origin; "blend" cargoes after STS | Common High Priority List HS codes (chips, CNC tools); goods outside the customer's line of business | Funds from mixers, darknet, ransomware or sanctioned exchanges; ruble stablecoin A7A5; USDT swaps |
| **How** | AIS gaps or spoofing; STS in hotspots (Gulf of Oman, off Malaysia, Greece, Malta); flag-hopping or false flags; renaming | Over- or under-invoicing; multiple invoices; phantom shipments; wrong HS codes; freight forwarder as consignee | Chain-hopping and bridges; peel chains; rapid fiat out after crypto in; fan-in/fan-out to many accounts |
| **Paper** | Missing or generic price-cap attestation; non-itemised freight and insurance; non-IG P&I insurer; origin conflicts with voyage | End-use claims that don't fit the product; reused or vague bills of lading; no end-user certificate | No Travel Rule data; VASP can't explain nested customers; IP or device in Russia, Iran or DPRK |
| **Money** | Charter or freight paid via unrelated third countries; round sums; payments to newly formed traders in UAE/HK | Payer ≠ buyer; exchange-house funding; circular flows among related traders; many small payments to similar suppliers | Crypto-sourced inflows paying dual-use importers; stablecoin conversions inconsistent with income |

### Universal flags (all three)

- **Opaque ownership:** nominees, trusts or law firms recorded as beneficial owner (Project 02: 136 such records in one designated Cypriot network).
- **Shared infrastructure** with a designated party: same address, director, ship manager, IP or wallet cluster.
- **Recent formation plus sudden high volume** in a sensitive sector.
- **Third-country routing** with no commercial logic (UAE, Türkiye, Armenia, Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan, Hong Kong, Cyprus).
- **Name or address similar** to a listed party. Check identifiers, because common names cause false positives.

### Screening checklist

1. Screen **all parties + identifiers**: names, IMO numbers, wallet addresses, addresses, registration numbers.
2. Apply the **OFAC 50% Rule**: aggregate blocked ownership ≥50% means blocked, even if unlisted.
3. Check **FinCEN special measures** (§311/§9714) as hard stops, separately from OFAC.
4. Note the **regime in force**: price cap level and licences differ by jurisdiction (US $60; EU/UK $44.10 from Feb 2026) and change often.
5. **Re-screen the book** after every mass designation (e.g. 183 vessels on 10 Jan 2025).
6. **Document** the disposition: what matched, what was checked, why it was cleared or escalated.
7. SAR key terms: `FIN-2022-RUSSIASANCTIONS` · `FIN-2022-RUSSIABIS` · `CMLN-2025-A003` (where applicable).
